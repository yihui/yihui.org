For two and a half years, from January 2024 to June 2026, Posit paid me as a
contractor to maintain the R Markdown ecosystem (**rmarkdown**, **knitr**,
**bookdown**, **blogdown**, **pagedown**, etc.). That contract was terminated in
June this year. They offered to take the maintainership from me, but I chose to
continue the maintenance on my own, because I didn't want these packages to
enter maintenance mode yet as they suggested (which means doing only the bare
minimum work to keep them afloat on CRAN). At the same time, I agreed that if I
could no longer maintain these packages someday, Posit could take over the
maintainership, since I completely trust my former colleague [Christophe
Dervieux](https://github.com/cderv) and I believe these packages will be well
taken care of in his hands.

At this point, I'm still maintaining the R Markdown ecosystem. I'm just no
longer paid to do so. Posit has kindly offered me sponsorship on GitHub for the
next five years, which I truly appreciate (and thanks for the previous 2.5 years
of financial support!). The sponsorship, if converted to time at my current
hourly rate, is about 20 hours of work a year, which won't be enough for the
work.

That brings up the question: should I ask for sponsorship from the
community?[^1] I have more and stronger reasons for "No" than "Yes":

[^1]: I saw the post [Should Academics Promote
    Themselves?](https://broniatowski.substack.com/p/should-academics-promote-themselves)
    by David Broniatowski the other day, which describes a similar struggle.

| Why "No" | Why "Yes" |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| I have contract jobs at Merck and Swiss Re, and I'm not too worried about my income (yet). I can afford the extra unpaid work. | I have 3 kids who haven't started burning my money wildly yet (currently the obvious winner is the 2-year old---\$20K a year for daycare). I also support some family members financially in China. |
| A great many (perhaps the vast majority) of open-source software developers are unpaid and [even unnoticed](https://xkcd.com/2347/). | I can have the extra money to sponsor or donate to these unpaid developers (e.g., [Tomas Kalibera](https://www.r-project.org/foundation/kalibera.html)). |
| R Markdown appears to be less and less important as more people switch to Quarto, and my work will be less valuable. | Perhaps my work in [knitr](/en/2026/09/knitr-news/) and [rmarkdown](/en/2026/09/rmarkdown-news/) still has some value. I just don't know how much. |
| Even if no one pays me a cent, I'll still do the work anyway. I love solving problems and helping people. | How about using the extra money to pay someone to free me from some daily chores so I can devote the time to the open-source work instead? Does anyone know that I have to check 26,000+ packages before I release new versions of knitr and rmarkdown to CRAN? |
| My sponsors/donors actually need the money more than me. | Perhaps I should seek corporate or organizational sponsorship instead of individual contributions? I'm not sure how many people would like me to "[shut up and take their money](https://bsky.app/profile/milesmcbain.bsky.social/post/3mj4det6ua22y)"\... |
| I don't want my mind to be too closely attached to money. Once I receive it, I'll naturally experience loss aversion later when the "free" money no longer flows to me, although it's not too hard for me to overcome it. |  |
| If I really want to make money, I should start another business instead, and then I wouldn't need to struggle at all. |  |
